Reported content

What listeners flagged as harmful in FAM - other people's comments, vibes, profiles, group chats and episodes - for you to take down or leave up. Apple requires it of any app where people post (App Store guideline 1.2): a way to report, a way to block, and an answer to every report within 24 hours. Oldest open first, so the top of the list is the next one due. Who reported is never shown.

Episode quality

Every written episode scored on six things and filed by the category tree (PROBLEMS.md §309). Code scores are proxies measured on every episode, 0 to 1. Grade (g) is the model grader's, not used for ranking until it agrees with your own scores. Listeners (l): for the hook, the share who stayed past twenty seconds; for storytelling, the share who finished. Nodes are sorted by episodes × how far below their own heading they fall; open one for its weakest episodes.

Does each measure predict listening?

Rank correlation between each measure and how much of an episode its listeners heard. One that predicts nothing is a measure to reword, not a number to optimise.

What went wrong, and where

Every stage of making an episode records what went wrong here (§269). Errors mean somebody heard less than they should have, or nothing; notices mean a guard did its job or something degraded and the episode still played. Open any row for what it means, which categories of episode it hit, what time of day it happens and the newest cases. Last 7 days; no listener is named; the menu's badge is errors in the last hour.

The stages

The brief (episode intelligence timed out, ran out of room, failed, or one half did not answer), live providers (BALLDONTLIE, Finnhub, Polymarket, weather broke, timed out, or answered too late - never "no such game", which is an answer), research (a search backend failed, or every rung came back empty and the episode had no sources), the writer (failed, refused, or a check removed a sentence before it was spoken), the editions (a Trending or DailyFAM episode could not be written) the episode itself (no voice, failed before any audio, no speech, broke mid-stream, or the voice fell behind) and the voice worker (no worker can speak, or the worker stopped registering its address), checked once a minute whether or not anybody is listening.

Newest

Every error and notice, newest first, in your own clock.

Timing and health, by step

How long each step of making an episode takes and how often it fails at its job (§270), whether or not anything went wrong - one row per step and per who was waiting: listener waiting (a search or a tap, in front of the first word), ahead (prefetch and the editions, nobody waiting) or listener (the whole wait from tap to first audio). Failed counts a timeout, an error, a refusal, running out of room, an unreadable answer, a part that came too late, a search with no sources, or a live provider that broke or answered stale; how it ended lists every ending, including the ones a step is built to have (a cut not made, no such game). Times are seconds; averages and percentiles are over the last 7 days unless marked 24h. Every server process writes here, and a redeploy keeps it.

Refusals: calls the model declined

Every model call a safety classifier declined (§273) - the writer, each part of the brief, and the evidence cut. Haiku 5.5 has no fallback: a declined writer speaks the fixed line "I can't put together a briefing on that" in place of the episode, a declined brief part searches the question as typed, and a declined cut keeps the whole packet. No retry on another model is built yet; this is the record of what one would have to rescue. Category and reason are the classifier's own (some declines give none); spoken first is how many sentences the listener had already heard when the writer stopped. Last 7 days, newest first.

Outside services, requests per day

Every request that went out to each data service, counted where it was sent (a cached answer is never counted; a refused one is). Days are UTC, when GNews resets. The limit is today's; in parentheses, what the next paid plan raises it to. BALLDONTLIE is one plan for every league, so each league it was asked about has a row of its own under it, saying where the requests went. When to buy each is in docs/SCALING_TIMELINE.md.

Live routing, per written episode

Every episode is searched; a live provider (BALLDONTLIE, Finnhub, Polymarket, weather) is asked beside the search only when the brief names a live domain. Each written episode is one of: answered (a provider's facts reached the writer), routed, no answer (asked, and why not), not routed (the brief named no domain) or not routed, EI degraded (there was no brief). Suspect marks a not-routed question that names a team this server has a catalogue for, a named broadcast slot or a market word - worth a look. Since §265 code also routes a question the brief left empty when it names a major-league team, a named game, or a market word with a listed company; Routed by says whether the brief or code did. Last 7 days; every written episode has a row (searches, editions, prefetched scripts), a cached replay none. Prefetch never asks a provider, so a prefetched script reads as asked-and-unanswered or not routed.

How names are said

The voice reads spelling and nothing else, so a name like Sagapolutele is respelled before it is spoken. Episode intelligence and the writer add names as they meet them; yours are never replaced by theirs. Only the voice hears these - captions and titles keep the real spelling.

Checked against WikipediaEvery name a model adds is looked up on Wikipedia in the background; where Wikipedia says it differently, Wikipedia's version replaces the model's. Yours are never replaced - Wikipedia's reading is shown beside them.
