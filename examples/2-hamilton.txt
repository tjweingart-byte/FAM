how did hamilton become a hit

Hamilton began as a book bought for a holiday. In 2008, Lin-Manuel Miranda, already the writer and star of the Broadway musical In the Heights, picked up Ron Chernow's seven-hundred-page biography of Alexander Hamilton before a trip to Mexico, and a few chapters in he was hearing it as hip-hop.

His pitch was that Hamilton's life already was a rap story: an orphan from the Caribbean who wrote his way out of poverty, talked faster than everyone in the room, and died because he couldn't stop arguing. Miranda started with a single song, the one that would open the show.

He first performed it in public at the White House, at a poetry and music evening in May 2009. He told the room he was working on a concept album about someone who embodied hip-hop - the Treasury Secretary, Alexander Hamilton - and the audience laughed. Then he performed what became the show's opening number, and the room, the President among them, applauded. The video spread online, and it became the proof the idea worked.

It took him six more years to finish. Hamilton opened at the Public Theater in New York in early 2015, sold out its run, and moved to Broadway that summer, with a cast of mostly Black and Latino actors playing the founding fathers. That casting was the argument of the show: America's origin story, told by the people who look like America now.

Tickets became almost impossible to get. In 2016 it won the Pulitzer Prize for Drama, and it set a record with sixteen Tony nominations, winning eleven - all from a biography he took to the beach.
