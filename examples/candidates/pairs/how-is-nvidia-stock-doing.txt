how is Nvidia stock doing

BEFORE:
NVIDIA's shares closed Friday at $162.85 on the Nasdaq, down two point zero one percent on the day. That is the figure to hold onto while trading runs this Monday afternoon. Friday was a choppy session. The stock opened near $170 and touched a high of $170.00, then slid to $158.39 before recovering part of the drop into the close. It was not a lone move. AMD fell almost nine percent that day, Broadcom nearly five, Taiwan Semiconductor under two, and the Nasdaq-100 fund fell too. The bigger drop came on Thursday. A Motley Fool market report from that day led with a headline saying Nvidia's CEO had made a stunning exit, and that it sent Wall Street lower while rivals surged. That headline is the reason on offer for Thursday's slide. It still sits well short of its 52-week high of $243.37. Its 52-week low is $148.80, so at $162.85 it is in the lower part of a very wide range. The last settled price is $162.85, and it is still about a third below the high it reached over the past year.

AFTER:
Nvidia stock closed on Friday the thirteenth of November at a hundred and sixty-two dollars and eighty-five cents, after falling two days running.

The heavier fall was Thursday's: seven dollars and fifty-four cents, a little over four percent, in one session. A Motley Fool market report that day tied it to what its headline called the stunning exit of Nvidia's chief executive, which it said sent Wall Street lower while rivals surged.

But a day later the rivals were falling too. Nvidia opened Friday near a hundred and seventy dollars and sank as low as a hundred and fifty-eight thirty-nine, though it won part of that back to end the day down two percent. AMD lost almost nine percent in the same session, so by then it was a bad day for chipmakers, not only for Nvidia.

Over the past twelve months the stock has been as high as two hundred and forty-three dollars and as low as a hundred and forty-eight eighty. Friday's close is fourteen dollars above that low, and eighty below that high.
