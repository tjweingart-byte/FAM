why is a marathon 26.2 miles

A marathon is twenty-six point two miles because of where a royal box was sitting at the London Olympics in 1908.

The race was named after the legend of a Greek messenger running from the battlefield at Marathon to Athens, and when it was revived for the first modern Olympics in 1896 it was about twenty-five miles - roughly the distance of that run. For the next few Games it stayed near that, give or take, set by whatever course the host city laid out. Nobody thought the exact number mattered.

Then London. The organisers wanted the race to start at Windsor Castle, so it could begin within sight of the royal nursery, and to finish in the new Olympic stadium at White City - not anywhere on the track, but in front of the royal box, where Queen Alexandra was sitting. Measured from the castle lawn to that spot in the stadium, the course came out at twenty-six miles and three hundred and eighty-five yards.

That race also became the most famous marathon anyone had seen. The Italian runner Dorando Pietri entered the stadium first, collapsed several times on the track, ran the wrong way, and was helped across the line by officials - and was disqualified for it. The drama made the marathon a sensation, and races sprang up everywhere copying the London distance.

For a while the length still varied from race to race. It was only in 1921 that athletics' governing body fixed the official distance, and the number it chose was London's: twenty-six miles, three hundred and eighty-five yards. Forty-two point one nine five kilometres. Every marathon run since is the distance from a castle lawn to a queen's seat.
