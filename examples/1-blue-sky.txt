why is the sky blue

Sunlight arrives white - every colour at once - and the air bends some of those colours out of its path far more than others. The molecules of nitrogen and oxygen are tiny, much smaller than a wavelength of light, and something that small scatters short waves far more strongly than long ones. Blue light, with its short wave, gets knocked sideways several times more than red.

So blue is thrown around the whole sky, bouncing from molecule to molecule until it reaches you from every direction at once. Wherever you look up, away from the sun, you are seeing that scattered blue.

Violet is shorter still and scatters even more, but the sun sends less of it and your eyes are far less sensitive to it, so the mix reads as blue.

At sunset the light reaches you sideways, through much more air. By the time it arrives, most of the blue has been scattered off somewhere else, and what is left is the red and orange.
