how did max verstappen do in the singapore sprint and what did mclaren's collision change

Max Verstappen won the Singapore sprint on Saturday the tenth of October by two and a half seconds from Lewis Hamilton - from pole, after being beaten off the line, because the driver who beat him, George Russell, threw the whole race away inside the first lap.

Half the track was wet and half was drying, and in those conditions a clean launch is worth more than raw pace. Russell's was cleaner. He took the lead into Turn One and looked gone. Then, at the final corner of the opening lap, he ran too wide over the kerb, lost the rear, speared into the inside wall and bounced back across the track. Nobody hit him, which at those closing speeds was its own small miracle, but his race was over and the safety car came out.

Verstappen inherited the lead clean, no passing required. From the restart he managed it - intermediate tyres on a drying track, Hamilton and Charles Leclerc chasing, and the grip changing under all of them lap by lap. By his own admission afterwards his start had been "horrendous" again; he won on Russell's mistake and his own tyre management, not on pace off the line.

The other story was McLaren colliding with itself on the final lap. Lando Norris went for Oscar Piastri at Turn Five, got alongside legitimately, Piastri held his line, the cars touched and both spun. Piastri hit the barrier and was out; Norris kept going but fell to eighth. The stewards called it a racing incident between team-mates and handed out no penalty.

The real winner of that crash was Mercedes' Kimi Antonelli, who inherited fourth from a collision he was nowhere near - on the afternoon his own team-mate, the man who had led at the first corner, scored nothing.
